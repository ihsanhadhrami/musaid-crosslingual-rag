A budget doesn't have to be complicated. The aim is simple: know where your money goes, and make sure the important things are paid first.

## The 50/30/20 idea

One popular approach divides your take-home pay into three parts:

- **About half for needs:** rent, bills, food, transport, school fees and money you send to family.
- **About a third for wants:** eating out, shopping, entertainment.
- **About a fifth for savings and goals:** your emergency fund, Hajj or Umrah, a car, a wedding.

These are starting points, not rules. If you support family at home, your "needs" may be much bigger, and that's fine.

## Use Pockets for each part

Create a pocket for bills and one for each savings goal. On salary day, move the money in, or set up automatic transfers. What's left in your main balance is what you can spend. See [Budgeting with Pockets](kb-0259).

## Keep the spending part on your card

Using your card or phone for everyday spending means every payment is in **Activity**, with the merchant's name. At the end of the month, filter **Activity** by card payments to see where the money went.

## When salary day is late

If your salary arrives late because of a weekend or public holiday, a scheduled transfer on the same day is retried later that day, then skipped if there still isn't enough money. Schedule rent and family transfers a day or two after your usual salary date. See [Scheduled and recurring transfers](kb-0051).

## Review it

Once a month, compare what you planned with what happened, and adjust. See [A monthly money check-up](kb-0334).
