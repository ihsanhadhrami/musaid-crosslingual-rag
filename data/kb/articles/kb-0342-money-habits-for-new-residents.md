Moving to Saudi Arabia or the UAE for work is exciting, and there's a lot to set up at once. These steps help you get your finances in order from the start.

## Verify your account early

You'll need your Iqama in Saudi Arabia, or your Emirates ID in the UAE, to verify your Sahla Pay account. Until you're verified, your limits are low and you can't send money abroad. See [Documents required for verification in Saudi Arabia](kb-0003) and [Documents required for verification in the UAE](kb-0004).

Visitors on a tourist or visit visa can't open an account, so wait until your residence documents are ready.

## Getting your salary

Ask your employer whether they can pay your salary into your Sahla Pay IBAN. In the UAE, salaries paid through the Wage Protection System can't go directly to Sahla Pay. If that applies to you, have your salary paid to your bank and transfer what you need. See [Receiving your salary in Sahla Pay](kb-0252).

## Sending money home

Add your family's bank details once, carefully, and save them as a recipient. Read the guide for your home country before your first transfer; each one lists the details banks there need and the common mistakes.

## Keep your documents up to date

When your Iqama or Emirates ID is renewed, upload the new one straight away. If your ID expires and the grace period ends, payments from your account are paused until you upload a valid one. See [Updating an expired ID or Iqama](kb-0011).

## Build a cushion

Start an emergency fund, even a small one, before you start sending large amounts home. Unexpected costs come up in the first months in a new country. See [Building an emergency fund](kb-0329).

## Watch out for scams

New residents are a target for fake job offers, fake government fines and calls claiming your visa or account has a problem. No government office will ask for a one-time password.
