When you pay or send money in another currency, two things decide how much it costs: the exchange rate, and any fees.

## The reference rate and the markup

Exchange rates change all the time. We start from a reference rate taken from the currency markets, then add a markup. The rate the app shows already includes the markup, so there's no separate charge for it. The markup is different in Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Egypt. See [Foreign exchange markup](kb-0139).

## Card payments

For card payments abroad, the rate is applied when the card network settles the payment, which can be a day or two after you pay. The amount in **Activity** can change slightly when the payment is settled.

## International transfers

For transfers, the rate is fixed when you confirm. The amount your recipient gets doesn't change, even if the market moves while the transfer is on its way.

If the receiving bank sends the money back, it's converted again at the rate on the day it's returned, so you may get back slightly more or less than you sent.

## Refunds

Refunds in a foreign currency are converted at the rate on the day of the refund, not the day of the payment. See [Refunds in a different currency](kb-0091).

## Tips

- Compare the final amount, not just the rate, when you look at other providers.
- Don't wait for the perfect rate. Rates go up and down, and nobody can predict them reliably.
- Always pay in the local currency when you're abroad.
