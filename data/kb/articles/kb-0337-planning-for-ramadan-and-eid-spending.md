Ramadan and Eid are a time for family, generosity and celebration, and for many households they're also the most expensive weeks of the year. A little planning keeps the month about what matters.

## Before Ramadan starts

Make a list of what you expect to spend more on:

- Food for iftar and suhoor, especially if you host family and friends.
- Zakat and sadaqah.
- Eid clothes and gifts.
- Eidiya for children in the family.
- Travel to visit family, or flights home.

Put a rough amount next to each. Then compare the total with what you can afford without touching your emergency fund.

## Give each one a pocket

Create a pocket for Ramadan spending and one for Eid. Move money in on salary day before Ramadan, so it's set aside before everyday spending starts. See [Budgeting with Pockets](kb-0259).

## Shopping

Prices for some foods go up in the first days of Ramadan. Buying long-lasting items, like rice, dates, oil and flour, a couple of weeks before can save money and time. Use one card for food shopping so you can see the total in **Activity**.

## Eidiya

Decide on amounts before Eid and send them in one go with **Add more people**. Every transfer counts towards your daily limit separately, so if you're sending to many people, check your limit first, or send over two days. See [Ramadan and Eid: sending Eidiya and managing busy days](kb-0249).

## Charity

Give through registered charities. Be careful with donation requests that arrive by WhatsApp or social media and ask you to send money to an individual's account. Fraudsters use Ramadan to collect fake donations.

## After Eid

Look at what you actually spent, and write down anything you'd do differently next year. Next Ramadan, your list will be much quicker to make.
