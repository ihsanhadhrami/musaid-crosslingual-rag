A refund looks simple: the merchant gives you your money back. In practice, it passes through several systems, which is why it can take longer than you expect.

## The merchant has to start it

Only the merchant can issue a refund for a card or QR payment. We can't take money back from a merchant just because you asked us to, unless you have grounds for a dispute. So the first step is always to ask the merchant. See [Requesting a refund from a merchant](kb-0074).

## The merchant's side

When the merchant agrees, it sends the refund through its own bank. Merchants process refunds at their own speed. Some do it immediately, others in a weekly batch. This part can take a week or more, and we can't see it.

## The card network

The merchant's bank sends the refund through the card network to us. Once it reaches us, we credit it within the times in [Refund timelines](kb-0071). These times start when the merchant issues the refund, not when you asked for it.

## QR payments

QR refunds don't go through the card network. They're credited to your balance as soon as the merchant sends them.

## If your card has changed

A refund follows your Sahla Pay account, not the card. It still reaches your balance if the card you paid with has expired, been replaced or been closed. If your whole account has been closed, see [Refunds after account closure](kb-0088).

## If the amount is different

A refund in a foreign currency is converted on the day it arrives, so it can be a little more or less than you paid. Partial refunds are also common, for example when you return only some items. See [Partial refunds](kb-0083).

## When to contact us

If the merchant has given you a refund reference, such as an ARN, and the time in our refund timelines has passed, contact us with the reference. We can trace the refund through the card network.
