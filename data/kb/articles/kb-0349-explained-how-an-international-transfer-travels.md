An international transfer can go through several banks before it reaches your recipient. Knowing the journey helps you understand the statuses in the app and why some transfers take longer.

## 1. You confirm

When you confirm, we take the amount and the fee from your balance and fix the exchange rate. The transfer shows **Pending**, or **Under review** if it needs a security check first.

## 2. We send it to our partner

We send the money through our partner bank or payment network for that country. For many countries, the money is converted and paid out locally. For others, it travels through the international banking network to the recipient's bank, sometimes through one or more intermediary banks.

When the money has left us, the status changes to **Sent to bank**.

## 3. The receiving bank

The receiving bank checks the details, especially the account number and name, and credits the account. Banks in some countries are strict about names, and a small difference, like a missing middle name, can lead the bank to return the transfer.

Delivery times are counted until the transfer is **Sent to bank**. Time the receiving bank takes for its own checks is outside our control. Destination guides give the usual times for each country. For example, see [Sending money to India](kb-0217).

## Why transfers are delayed

- Weekends and public holidays in either country.
- Checks at an intermediary or receiving bank.
- Details that need correcting.

## Why transfers come back

If the receiving bank rejects the transfer, for example because the account is closed or the name doesn't match, the money comes back to your balance, including our fee, converted at the exchange rate on the day it's returned. Intermediary banks may have taken a fee on the way, which we can't refund.

## Recalls

Once the money has reached the receiving bank, we can only ask for it back with a recall, which has a fee, and the receiving bank decides whether to return it. See [Disputes for international transfers](kb-0095).
