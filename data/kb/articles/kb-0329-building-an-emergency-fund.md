An emergency fund is money you keep aside for the unexpected: a car repair, a medical bill, a gap between jobs, or a flight home at short notice. Having it means a surprise doesn't turn into debt.

## How much do you need?

A common rule of thumb is three to six months of essential spending: rent, bills, food, transport and any money you send to family. If that sounds like a lot, start smaller. Even one month's rent set aside makes a real difference.

Work out your essential monthly spending from your statements. In **Profile** > **Statements**, download the last three months as CSV and add up the payments you couldn't do without.

## Where to keep it

Keep it separate from the money you spend every day, so you don't use it by accident, but easy to reach when you need it.

A pocket called "Emergency" works well. Money in a pocket isn't used for card payments or transfers until you move it back to your main balance, which takes a second. See [Budgeting with Pockets](kb-0259).

Keep in mind that money in Pockets counts towards your balance limit, and it doesn't earn interest or profit. If your emergency fund grows beyond what your Sahla Pay account can hold, keep the rest in a bank savings account.

## Building it up

- **Pay yourself first.** Set an automatic transfer into the pocket on salary day, before you spend anything else.
- **Save windfalls.** Put part of any bonus, gift or refund straight into the pocket.
- **Round up.** Move a small amount into the pocket after every week where you spent less than planned.

## When to use it

Only for real emergencies, things that are urgent, necessary and unexpected. A sale, a holiday or a new phone isn't an emergency; give those their own pocket instead.

When you use it, top it back up as soon as you can, before you go back to saving for other goals.
