When money changes currency, through an international transfer or a card payment in another currency, we convert it at our exchange rate.

## How our rate works

Our rate is a reference market rate plus a markup:

| Country | Markup |
|---|---|
| Saudi Arabia | {{fees.fx_markup.SA}} |
| UAE | {{fees.fx_markup.AE}} |
| Egypt | {{fees.fx_markup.EG}} |

The markup is included in the rate you see, not charged separately.

## When is the rate set?

For international transfers, when you confirm. The rate is locked, and the recipient gets exactly the amount shown.

For card payments, when the payment is completed by the card network, which can be a day or two after you paid. The amount can differ slightly from what you saw at the till.

## Should I pay in my own currency abroad?

When a shop or ATM abroad offers to charge you in your home currency, choose the local currency instead. The shop's conversion is usually more expensive than ours.

Refunds in a foreign currency are converted back at the rate on the day of the refund, so you may get back a little more or less than you paid. See [Refunds in a different currency](kb-0091).
