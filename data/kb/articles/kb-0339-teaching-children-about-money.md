Children learn about money long before they have any of their own, from watching how the adults around them spend, save and talk about it. A few habits at home go a long way.

## Start with saving goals

Young children understand saving best when it's for something they can picture: a toy, a bike, a game. Help them set a goal and track it. A jar with a picture of the goal on it works well at first.

## Pocket money

Regular pocket money, even a small amount, gives children practice making choices. Agree what it's for, for example snacks and small treats, and let them make their own mistakes with it. Spending all of it on the first day and having nothing left at the end of the week teaches more than any lecture.

## Talking about digital money

Children see adults pay with a phone and may think money is unlimited. Show them your balance going down when you pay. Explain that a card or phone payment is real money, just like cash.

## Teenagers

Teenagers can start to manage a small budget: phone credit, transport, outings with friends. This is also the time to talk about scams, especially messages offering prizes, gaming credits or easy money for "helping" move money.

## Accounts for young people

You need to be at least {{kyc.min_age.SA}} to open a Sahla Pay account in Saudi Arabia and {{kyc.min_age.AE}} in the UAE. In Egypt, young people can open their own account from {{kyc.min_age.EG}}, with a parent or guardian approving it until they're {{kyc.guardian_consent_under.EG}}. See [Helping a young family member in Egypt manage money](kb-0258).

Nobody can open an account in someone else's name, including for their children, and children shouldn't use a parent's account, even with permission.

## Lead by example

Talk about money decisions at home: why you're saving for something, how you compare prices, why you're waiting before buying. Children copy what they see.
